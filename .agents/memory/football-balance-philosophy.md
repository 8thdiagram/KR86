---
name: Football balance philosophy
description: The user's intended relationship between play selection, player ability and actual football execution.
---

Good play call + good players + good execution should improve the probability of success. A good defensive counter + good defenders + good execution should improve the probability of stopping the play. Neither side should receive guaranteed outcomes solely from play selection.

**Why:** The user wants meaningful rock-paper-scissors anticipation before the snap and entertaining arcade execution during the play, not predetermined outcomes or rigid NFL statistical reproduction.

**How to apply:** Judge proposed football balance changes using actual gameplay outcomes, personnel differences and execution together. Inside runs should sometimes produce a usable blocking lane, several yards or a secondary breakthrough, while a correctly anticipated run defense should reduce those opportunities rather than automatically stop every run.

When diagnosing breakaways, isolate the first secondary engagement from later re-blocking and inspect positions before changing pursuit.

**Why:** Controlled diagnostics showed that conspicuous late re-blocking can occur after the initial hold has already made the runner uncatchable. Removing only those later holds did not change the observed outcomes.

**How to apply:** Use matched first-hold/re-hold interventions and same-state secondary checkpoints to distinguish causes from visible symptoms. Revalidate the finding after engine changes rather than assuming it applies to every roster or starting position.

Validate spatial diagnostics independently of unchanged game outcomes.

**Why:** An observer can reproduce every score and random-state result while measuring positions in the wrong attacking direction. That can silently invalidate goal-side and recovery counts.

**How to apply:** Normalize attacking direction before interpreting milestones. Check milestone times and defender-side counts against independent traces for both field orientations, in addition to complete-state outcome replays.

Preserving a goal-side position is not the same as preserving a useful tackle opportunity.

**Why:** Isolated last-line experiments retained defenders ahead of the runner but exposed separate lateral-prediction, close-down and responsibility-handoff failures. Fewer touchdowns alone did not establish the intended intermediate-gain behavior.

**How to apply:** Inspect actual contact and full positional traces after a breakthrough. Separate the initial alignment, maintained depth, lateral leverage, blocking and final close-down. Do not infer catch-up ability from a single milestone velocity: intervening legal burst phases can offset an elite defender's apparent cruising-speed advantage.

Use receiver-only hold ablations before attributing a blocking effect to receivers.

**Why:** Removing all DB holds also removes OL-to-DB blocking, which can overstate the WR/TE contribution.

**How to apply:** Keep the original contests and bodies, vary only successful WR/TE-to-DB hold duration, and compare matched seeds separately from the broader all-DB ablation.
